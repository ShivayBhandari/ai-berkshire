**What is good about this business, in one sentence:** a fibre network nobody else can lay along the railways, whose own margin is rising.

**A historical parallel.** ITI Ltd, a state telecom-equipment maker whose shift into government project work brought revenue without profit. RailTel is far healthier, but the direction is the same.

**The short case, as its best holder would write it.** "Revenue tripled in five years and free cash flow went from ₹294 crore to ₹44 crore. The growth is project work at a 4% margin, paid for by customers who take 175 days to pay and backed by bank guarantees that doubled in a year. The telecom business is good, but it is a third of revenue and shrinking as a share. The owner cannot even fill its board. At 24 times earnings and 190 times free cash flow, you are paying a technology multiple for a government contractor."

**My own biases on this name.** The 32% jump in telecom result is one year; I may be anchoring on it. The PE below its own median is against a history that includes the 2021-24 railway PSU bubble: the median since Sep 2023 alone is 39.4x.

**India-specific checks.** Related parties: the Railways are licensor, customer and owner. Auditor: unmodified report. Promoter pledge: not applicable. Regulated prices: the revenue share and licence fee. Single government customer: largely government across both segments. Land and approvals: the right of way depends on the Railways.

**Where am I most likely to be wrong, and why would a smart person be short?** Wrong if the telecom margin keeps rising and project work stays a pass-through: then profit compounds on little capital. A smart person is short because of the cash: 44% conversion does not support 24x.

## Step 6 — the long horizon

**Real shift or cycle?** A shift: data traffic and government digitisation grow for years.

**Closest historical parallel.** Neutral fibre owners with a privileged right of way, like pipeline-corridor telecom networks: good returns on the fibre, poor ones when they chase services.

**Addressable market.** Order book over ₹10,400 cr against revenue of ₹4,277 cr. The telecom segment grew 10.1% in FY26. Small orders keep arriving: ₹63.15 cr from Prasar Bharati (17 Sep), ₹14.09 cr (22 Sep) and ₹21.57 cr from CMPDI (26 Sep) (BSE filings).

**Value chain and pricing power.** Bandwidth: price-takers against large telcos. Projects: price-takers on tender.

**Concentration.** Customers: largely government.

**For India: build-out or one order book?** The national digital build-out, sold mostly to government.

**Looking back from twenty years out, a lasting business or a moment?** The fibre is lasting; the project arm could be a moment.

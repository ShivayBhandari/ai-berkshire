# Part 2 — the deep read

## Step 2 — the business

**One sentence.** A fibre network along the railway tracks that rents bandwidth, attached to a government IT-projects contractor.

**Revenue split** (AR note 52):

| Segment | FY26 revenue | FY26 result | Margin | FY25 revenue | FY25 result | Margin |

| Project work | 2,776.79 | 109.47 | 3.9% | 2,114.97 | 94.03 | 4.4% |

Telecom is 35% of revenue and 79% of segment result. Its result rose 32% in FY26. Project revenue rose 31% and its result 16%.

**Ten-year trend** (Scr standalone; ROE on average equity, computed):

| FY | Sales | OPM | Net margin | ROE |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2025 | 3,478 | 15% | 8.6% | 15.7% |
| 2026 | 4,277 | 15% | 8.1% | 16.2% |

Ten-year averages: OPM 23.5%, net margin 11.5%, ROE 12.6%. Sales grew 26.2% a year FY21-FY26, profit 19.8%. The margin halved as project work grew; ROE rose because the same equity now turns over far more revenue.

**One-time or repeat?** Telecom is recurring (leases and service contracts). Projects are one-time.

**Customer lock-in.** Moderate in telecom; none in projects.

**Margin against peers.** Railway PSU peers FY26 OPM: IRCTC 32%, RITES 24%, CONCOR 21%, RailTel 15% (Scr). A pure telecom-infrastructure peer was not compared.

**Operating leverage.** FY25 to FY26: revenue +23.0%, operating profit +19.3% (₹533 cr → ₹636 cr, sum of Scr quarters), profit +15.3%. Profit grows slower than revenue.

**What is good about this business, in one sentence:** a fibre network nobody else can lay along the railways, whose own margin is rising.

## Step 4 — how it fails

| Failure path | Rough likelihood | Damage |
|---|---|---|
| Project work keeps diluting margin and cash | high; already happening | OPM 30% (FY17) → 15% (FY26); FCF 44% of profit |
| A receivable write-off | medium | ₹2,052.95 cr owed, 175 days of revenue |

| Railways open track-side fibre to others | low | the core moat goes |

**A historical parallel.** Other government telecom arms that became contractors: ITI Ltd, a state telecom-equipment maker whose shift into government project work brought revenue without profit. RailTel is far healthier, but the direction is the same.

**The short case, as its best holder would write it.** "Revenue tripled in five years and free cash flow went from ₹294 crore to ₹44 crore. The growth is project work at a 4% margin, paid for by customers who take 175 days to pay and backed by bank guarantees that doubled in a year. The telecom business is good, but it is a third of revenue and shrinking as a share. At 24 times earnings and 190 times free cash flow, you are paying a technology multiple for a government contractor."

**My own biases on this name.** The 32% jump in telecom result is one year; I may be anchoring on it. The PE below its own median is against a history that includes the 2021-24 railway PSU bubble.

**India-specific checks.** Related parties: the Railways are licensor, customer and owner. Auditor: unmodified report. Promoter pledge: not applicable. Regulated prices: the revenue share and licence fee. Single government customer: largely government across both segments. Land and approvals: the right of way depends on the Railways.

**Where am I most likely to be wrong, and why would a smart person be short?** Wrong if the telecom margin keeps rising and project work stays a pass-through: then profit compounds on little capital. A smart person is short because of the cash: 44% conversion does not support 24x.

## Step 6 — the long horizon

**Real shift or cycle?** A shift: data traffic and government digitisation grow for years.

**Closest historical parallel.** Neutral fibre owners with a privileged right of way, like pipeline-corridor telecom networks: good returns on the fibre, poor ones when they chase services.

**Addressable market.** Order book over ₹10,400 cr against revenue of ₹4,277 cr. The telecom segment grew 10.1% in FY26.

**Value chain and pricing power.** Bandwidth: price-takers against large telcos. Projects: price-takers on tender.

**Concentration.** Customers: largely government.

**For India: build-out or one order book?** The national digital build-out, sold mostly to government.

**Looking back from twenty years out, a lasting business or a moment?** The fibre is lasting; the project arm could be a moment.

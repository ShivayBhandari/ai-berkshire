# Part 2 — the deep read

## Step 2 — the business

**One sentence.** India's sole-source builder and maintainer of military aircraft, paid in advance by its only real customer, and earning interest on that advance.

**Revenue split.** Manufacturing and repair-and-overhaul (ROH). The FY26 orders show the mix: over ₹69,600 cr of manufacturing contracts and over ₹17,300 cr of ROH (AR). Exports are small (two Hindustan-228s to Guyana in FY26).

**Ten-year trend** (Scr consolidated; ROE on average equity, computed):

| FY | Sales | OPM | Net margin | ROE |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2017 | 17,950 | 18% | 14.6% | 22.3% |
| 2018 | 18,520 | 19% | 10.7% | 18.0% |
| 2019 | 20,008 | 23% | 11.6% | 21.6% |
| 2020 | 21,445 | 23% | 13.4% | 22.8% |
| 2021 | 22,755 | 24% | 14.2% | 22.6% |
| 2022 | 24,620 | 22% | 20.6% | 29.3% |
| 2023 | 26,927 | 25% | 21.6% | 27.2% |
| 2024 | 30,381 | 32% | 25.1% | 28.9% |
| 2025 | 30,981 | 31% | 27.0% | 26.1% |
| 2026 | 33,089 | 30% | 27.5% | 24.0% |

Ten-year averages: OPM 24.7%, net margin 18.7%, ROE 24.3%. Sales grew 7.8% a year FY21-FY26; profit 23.0%.

**Where the profit growth came from.** FY24 to FY26, operating profit went from ₹9,752 cr to ₹9,788 cr, +0.4%. Other income went from ₹1,923 cr to ₹3,743 cr. Interest on bank deposits alone was ₹2,967 cr in FY26. The deposits are funded mainly by ₹73,936 cr of customer advances and milestone receipts.

**One-time or repeat?** Manufacturing is lumpy, contract by contract; ROH and spares repeat for the life of each fleet.

**Customer lock-in.** Very high: the armed forces cannot get HAL's platforms overhauled elsewhere.

**Margin against peers.** FY26 OPM: HAL 30%, BEL 29% (Scr). BDL's ten-year average OPM is 17.7% (Defence screen). HAL's ten-year ROE (24.3%) is the highest of the three.

**Operating leverage.** FY25 to FY26: revenue +6.8%, operating profit +1.7%, net profit +9.0%. Profit grows faster than revenue only because of other income.

**What is good about this business, in one sentence:** customers pay in advance for years of work that nobody else in India can do.

## Step 4 — how it fails

| Failure path | Rough likelihood | Damage |
|---|---|---|
| Operating profit stays flat while the multiple assumes growth | high; already three years | a de-rating from 34x toward the high teens |
| Interest income falls as advances are used up on deliveries | medium | ₹2,967 cr of FY26 interest; about a quarter of pre-tax profit |
| 4th price revision lands below the provisional prices booked since FY24 | medium | a reversal of revenue already booked; size not disclosed |
| Tejas Mk1A deliveries slip past Mar 2027 on engines | medium | revenue timing; penalties or customer pressure |
| Ministry moves new programmes to competitive procurement | medium over ten years | lower margins on new work |
| Onerous contracts or crash liabilities | low-medium | ₹843.36 cr provision for a replacement Su-30 in FY25 (AR) |

**A historical parallel.** Bharat Dynamics, 2024-2026: a sole-source defence PSU re-rated to a high multiple, then its profit fell two years running (₹613 cr in FY24 → ₹420 cr in FY26) and ROE nearly halved, 17.9% → 10.2% (Defence screen). Sole-source does not protect the margin from the customer.

**The short case, as its best holder would write it.** "Operating profit has not grown in three years. Every rupee of profit growth since FY24 is interest on money the Ministry advanced, which HAL will spend as it delivers. Revenue for spares and services has been booked on provisional prices for three years while the price revision drags on. The flagship programme is waiting on American engines. And you are paying 34 times earnings, two and a half times the stock's own history, for a company whose customer sets its prices."

**My own biases on this name.** The order book is a big, round, reassuring number; I am in danger of treating it as profit. The Defence screen made HAL look cheap because every peer was dearer; against its own history it is not.

**India-specific checks.** Related parties: the Ministry is owner, customer and price-setter. Auditor: emphasis of matter, no qualification. Promoter pledge: not applicable. Regulated prices: FPQ policy. Single government customer: yes. Land and approvals: not material.

**Where am I most likely to be wrong, and why would a smart person be short?** Wrong if the order book converts at 30% margins from FY27, which could lift operating profit fast. A smart person is short because that has not happened for three years and the multiple already assumes it.

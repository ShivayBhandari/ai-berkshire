- **One sentence:** IEX runs electronic auctions where power buyers and sellers bid for electricity for the next day (DAM) or the next hour (RTM), and charges both sides a fee per unit traded, about 3.96 paise in total.
- **In ten years:** the same auctions, more of India's power traded through them, plus carbon credits, coal and gas (IGX).
- **The variables that decide it:** (1) whether CERC's market-coupling rule goes live, and for which segments; (2) whether the fee per unit holds once the exchanges share one price; (3) how fast exchange trading grows (141 BU, +17% in FY26).

Three stars: the business is simple, but the market design is being rewritten by the regulator, so ten years out is genuinely unclear.

# Part 2 — the deep read

## Step 2 — the business

**One sentence.** A toll booth on India's spot electricity market: about 4 paise on every unit traded, almost no cost, and almost all of the trading.

**Revenue split, FY26** (AR note 27): electricity transaction fees ₹558.51 cr (91%), certificate trading (RECs, ESCerts) ₹25.45 cr (4%, down from ₹35.21 cr), annual subscriptions ₹22.82 cr, membership and transfer fees ₹4.67 cr, I-REC support ₹3.75 cr. Within electricity, RTM is the growth: 24.2 BU (FY23) → 54.9 BU (FY26), and now 34% of volume. DAM was 95% of volume in FY16 and is 39% now (deck).

**Ten-year trend** (Scr standalone; ROE on average equity, computed):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2017 | 199 | 71.9% | 57.3% | 48.1% | 19.5% |
| 2018 | 230 | 80.4% | 57.4% | 47.1% | 13.0% |
| 2019 | 254 | 79.9% | 65.0% | 50.5% | 17.2% |
| 2020 | 257 | 79.4% | 69.3% | 46.8% | 17.5% |
| 2021 | 317 | 81.7% | 67.2% | 46.2% | 14.2% |
| 2022 | 426 | 85.9% | 71.1% | 49.1% | 13.0% |

Ten-year averages FY17-FY26: OPM 81.7%, net margin 69.2%, ROE 44.7%. Sales grew 13.6% a year FY16-FY26, profit 16.8%. Consolidated FY23-FY26: sales 15.4% a year, profit 17.2%. ROE fell from the high 40s to about 39% because cash piles up faster than it is paid out.

**One-time or repeat?** Repeat. The same members trade every day. A platform, not a product.

**How locked in is the customer?** By liquidity, not by contract. A seller who bids on PXIL today may not get cleared, because the buyers are on IEX. That is the whole lock-in, and it is exactly what coupling removes.

**Margin against named peers.** PXIL and HPX are unlisted and their accounts were not read, so no like-for-like margin is given. The screen's survivors run margins of 40-85%; IEX's 84.5% is at the top of that range.

**Operating leverage.** FY25 to FY26, consolidated: revenue +14.6%, operating profit +14.5%, net profit +14.9%. Costs are small (₹95.59 cr, +14.3%), so profit moves almost one for one with the fee line. The same works in reverse: a 10% fall in electricity fees (₹55.85 cr) takes 10.7% off FY26 operating profit of ₹520 cr.

**What is good about this business, in one sentence:** nearly every rupee of fee growth reaches profit, with no capital needed, as long as the trading stays on IEX.

**Wider or narrower than five years ago?** Wider in the market, narrower in the rules. RTM, launched in 2020, went to IEX almost entirely, and its share of the short-term power market rose (exchanges are now 60% of it, per CERC via the AR). But since July 2025 the regulator has moved, step by step, to pool the bids.

**What market coupling is, exactly** (CERC draft, 17 Apr 2026, and its explanatory memo):

| Item | What was ordered or drafted |
|---|---|
| 23 Jul 2025 order (8/SM/2025) | CERC staff to start the process for coupling the day-ahead market in phases. IEX says the order named January 2026; exchanges to run the matching in turn, with Grid India as backup. RTM "considered subsequently" |
| 17 Apr 2026 draft rule | Grid India becomes the single Market Coupling Operator. **Scope: DAM, RTM "and other market segments"**, from dates CERC notifies, possibly different per segment. Exchanges collect bids and send them to Grid India, which sets one price for everyone. Grid India writes the detailed procedure within six months of the rule being notified. The rule's start date is to be notified separately |
| CERC's reasoning | a four-month shadow test (Dec 2024 - Mar 2025) found DAM welfare up ₹38 cr (0.3%) and RTM ₹72 lakh (0.01%); prices differ across exchanges; one reference price; better use of transmission. CERC says exchanges "will compete on service quality, technology, reliability, and even transaction fees" |
| Where it stands on 24 Sep 2026 | not in force. CERC's list of notified regulations (read 24 Sep 2026) shows no Power Market (Second Amendment) Regulations, 2026; the latest notified entry is dated 7 Sep 2026 (REC amendment) |

## Step 6 — the long horizon

**Real shift or cycle?** A real shift. Renewable power swings through the day, and short-term trading is how states balance it. RTM grew 42% a year over five years (deck). The short-term market was 302 BU in FY26 against 238 BU in FY25 (CERC via AR), and exchanges handle 60% of it.

**Closest historical parallel.** European power exchanges after coupling: a much bigger traded market, with fees competed down and the incumbent still first.

**Addressable market, growth and ceiling.** Short-term trading is 13% of India's 1,848 BU of generation (AR). The CMD thinks 25% is possible in five to six years (analyst meet). Demand is forecast near 2,700 BU by FY32 (deck, citing CEA). So traded volume could roughly double in six years. The ceiling is long-term contracts, which will stay the backbone.

**Value chain and pricing power.** Generators → exchanges → state power companies and factories. The regulator holds the pricing power over the exchanges.

**Concentration.** One customer is 16.0% of revenue. Suppliers: none that matter.

**For India: build-out or one order book?** The country's power build-out, not one order book.

**Looking back from twenty years out, a lasting business or a moment?** Exchange trading of power is lasting. IEX's near-monopoly on it may turn out to have been a moment between 2008 and the coupling rule.

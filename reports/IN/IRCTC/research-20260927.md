**One sentence.** A government-licensed toll booth on Indian rail ticketing, attached to a low-margin catering and tourism business.

**What is good about this business, in one sentence:** a monopoly toll on rail ticketing that earns 83 paise of profit on every rupee of fee and needs almost no capital.

## Step 4 — how it fails

| Failure path | Rough likelihood | Damage |
|---|---|---|
| The Railways take a share of the convenience fee | medium; ordered once, on 29 Oct 2021, and withdrawn the same day | 50:50 would cut pre-tax profit by about 33.8% (estimate); the share fell 29% intraday last time |
| Railways dues go unpaid | medium | ₹389.89 cr over three years old; ₹35.80 cr defaulted; a write-off hits profit directly |
| Catering keeps diluting the margin | high | OPM fell from 36% (FY23) to 32% (FY26) and 28% in Q1 FY27 |
| Ticketing hits its ceiling | high over ten years | online share already 88.74%; growth falls to rail traffic, about 5% |
| **New: the ₹5 UPI charge from 15 Oct 2026 falls on IRCTC** | NA: who pays is not stated | a flat ₹5 per UPI rail payment above ₹2,000; the charge cannot be passed to passengers (Business Today, 25 Sep 2026). UPI is more than half of IRCTC bookings (Factly, via search summary). How many tickets are above ₹2,000 is NA. If IRCTC pays, it comes straight off the ticketing margin |
| Rail Neer profiteering case lost | low-medium | ₹50.41 cr notice, before GSTAT |
| GST advance ruling goes against it | low-medium | ₹335.95 cr of income awaiting a ruling |

**A historical parallel.** IRCTC itself on 29 Oct 2021: one ministry letter, a 29% fall in a morning, and a reversal by evening. For a longer parallel, Coal India: a government monopoly whose minority holders were paid dividends while the owner set the prices, and whose share went nowhere for a decade.

**The short case, as its best holder would write it.** "You are buying a toll booth you do not own. The owner is also the customer that owes you ₹1,870 crore, some of it for more than three years, and it already tried once to take half the toll. The ticketing business is nearly fully online, so its growth is now the growth of rail traffic. Everything else IRCTC does earns a tenth of the margin and is growing faster, so the margin only goes one way. Even at the lowest PE since listing, 8% growth only gets you 10% a year. And from next month a new payment charge may land on the toll itself."

**My own biases on this name.** "Monopoly" is a word that reads well; I am in danger of scoring the licence as IRCTC's asset when it is the Railways'. The PE at the bottom of its own range anchors me, but the range began at a 2019-21 bubble. And Shivay already marked it Conditional; I must not lean toward agreeing because of that.

**India-specific checks.** Related-party transactions: the Railways are the licensor, a supplier and the largest debtor; balance confirmations are not exchanged (auditor). Auditor: appointed by the C&AG each year; emphasis of matter, no qualification. Promoter pledge: not applicable (Government of India). Regulated prices: all main prices set by the Railways. Single government customer: effectively yes, in ticketing and catering. Land and approvals: not material.

**Where am I most likely to be wrong, and why would a smart person be short?** Most likely wrong on policy in either direction: a quiet decade means the stock compounds; one letter means it re-rates down. A smart person is short because the next order costs the Railways nothing.

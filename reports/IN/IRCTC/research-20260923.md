# Part 2 — the deep read

## Step 2 — the business

**One sentence.** A government-licensed toll booth on Indian rail ticketing, attached to a low-margin catering and tourism business.

**Revenue split, FY26** (AR):

| Segment | Revenue | Share | Segment profit | Margin | Profit growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Catering | 2,398.75 | 46.0% | 249.90 | 10.4% | −8.0% |
| Internet Ticketing | 1,535.51 | 29.4% | 1,267.92 | 82.6% | +7.5% |
| Tourism | 890.08 | 17.1% | 127.50 | 14.3% | +35.9% |
| Rail Neer | 390.52 | 7.5% | 55.66 | 14.3% | +20.7% |

Ticketing is 74.5% of segment profit on 29.4% of revenue. The faster-growing lines (catering +12.9%, tourism +19.5% in revenue) earn a fraction of its margin, so the group margin drifts down as they grow.

|---|---|---|---|---|

Ten-year averages: OPM 29.7%, net margin 23.8%, ROE 34.5%. Profit grew 18.1% a year FY20-FY26 and 11.5% a year FY23-FY26. The FY21 lockdown distorts anything that starts there.

**One-time or repeat?** Repeat: every train journey booked online pays the fee again. A service.

**Customer lock-in.** Total, in ticketing: there is no other official online seller. None in catering or tourism.

**Margin against peers.** No listed company does what ticketing does. Railway PSU peers: RailTel OPM 15%, RITES 24%, CONCOR 21% (Scr FY26). IRCTC's 32% is the highest, and its ticketing margin of 82.6% has no peer.

**Operating leverage.** FY25 to FY26: revenue +11.6%, EBITDA +8.3%, profit +6.0% (AR). Weak, because the growth came from catering and tourism.

**What is good about this business, in one sentence:** a monopoly toll on rail ticketing that earns 83 paise of profit on every rupee of fee and needs almost no capital.

## Step 4 — how it fails

| Failure path | Rough likelihood | Damage |
|---|---|---|
| The Railways take a share of the convenience fee | medium; ordered once, on 29 Oct 2021, and withdrawn the same day | 50:50 would cut pre-tax profit by roughly a third (estimate); the share fell 29% intraday last time |
| Railways dues go unpaid | medium | ₹389.89 cr over three years old; ₹35.80 cr defaulted; a write-off hits profit directly |
| Catering keeps diluting the margin | high | OPM fell from 36% (FY23) to 32% (FY26) and 28% in Q1 FY27 |
| Ticketing hits its ceiling | high over ten years | online share already 88.74%; growth falls to rail traffic, about 5% |
| Rail Neer profiteering case lost | low-medium | ₹50.41 cr notice, before GSTAT |
| GST advance ruling goes against it | low-medium | ₹335.95 cr of income awaiting a ruling |

**A historical parallel.** IRCTC itself on 29 Oct 2021: one ministry letter, a 29% fall in a morning, and a reversal by evening. For a longer parallel, Coal India: a government monopoly whose minority holders were paid dividends while the owner set the prices, and whose share went nowhere for a decade.

**The short case, as its best holder would write it.** "You are buying a toll booth you do not own. The owner is also the customer that owes you ₹1,870 crore, some of it for more than three years, and it already tried once to take half the toll. The ticketing business is nearly fully online, so its growth is now the growth of rail traffic. Everything else IRCTC does earns a tenth of the margin and is growing faster, so the margin only goes one way. The PE is at its lowest ever because the market has worked this out."

**My own biases on this name.** "Monopoly" is a word that reads well; I am in danger of scoring the licence as IRCTC's asset when it is the Railways'. The PE at the bottom of its own range anchors me, but the range began at a 2019-21 bubble.

**India-specific checks.** Related-party transactions: the Railways are the licensor, a supplier and the largest debtor; balance confirmations are not exchanged (auditor). Auditor: appointed by the C&AG each year; emphasis of matter, no qualification. Promoter pledge: not applicable (Government of India). Regulated prices: all main prices set by the Railways. Single government customer: effectively yes, in ticketing and catering. Land and approvals: not material.

**Where am I most likely to be wrong, and why would a smart person be short?** Most likely wrong on policy in either direction: a quiet decade means the stock compounds; one letter means it re-rates down. A smart person is short because the next order costs the Railways nothing.

**A historical parallel.** Shree itself, FY2021 to FY2025. Capacity and volume grew every year (volume 27.74 MT in FY22 to 36.06 MT in FY25), while net profit went from ₹2,290 cr (FY21) to ₹1,124 cr (FY25) and ROE from 16.0% to 5.3%. The low-cost producer's margin fell as the industry added capacity. For a global parallel: ACC and Ambuja under Holcim, large low-cost producers whose returns hovered near the cost of capital for a decade.

**The short case, as its best holder would write it.** "You are paying 49 times trailing earnings for a company whose profit grew 3% a year for nine years. The cost edge that justified the premium is gone: take out the treasury income and its EBITDA per tonne is no better than UltraTech's. It runs its plants at 63% and still talks about 80+ MTPA. Since FY22 it put ₹4,820 cr of new capital to work and got ₹543 cr of extra EBITDA. The top three executives take about 7% of the profit. And the tax department's fake-bill allegation was thrown out on a time bar, not answered."

**My own biases on this name.** I am anchored on "lowest-cost producer", the AR's own phrase, and it is less true than it was. The EV/EBITDA framing flatters Shree; I found it after the PE looked expensive, which is how a story gets rescued. And the 22 Sep checklist already passed it, so I start with a lean toward agreeing.

**India-specific checks.** Related-party transactions: arm's length, none material (AR). Auditor: B R Maheswari & Co LLP, no change or qualification found. Promoter pledge: nil. Regulated prices: none directly; freight and state mining levies are set by government. Single government customer: no. Land and approvals: capacity growth needs new limestone leases through state auctions. Contingent items: CCI ₹397.51 cr and ₹18.44 cr; customs ₹81.30 cr; the Rajasthan subsidy dispute (AR note 34).

**Where am I most likely to be wrong, and why would a smart person be short?** Most likely wrong on utilisation: if volume grows into the 69.3 MTPA already built, free cash flow rises sharply with almost no new capital, and the PE falls fast. A smart person is short because that has been the bull case for three years, and profit fell for two of them.

## Step 6 — the long horizon

**Real shift or cycle?** Both, as for UltraTech. The cycle: capacity outruns demand every five to six years (FY23-FY25 was the last trough). The shift: consolidation into two national producers, UltraTech and Adani, with Shree the largest independent.

**Closest historical parallel.** The US and European cement industries after consolidation: whoever had the lowest cost into the fastest-growing region earned the best returns, and they rarely beat the cost of capital by much.

**Addressable market, growth and ceiling.** Industry utilisation about 71% in FY26 (Shree AR). Industry volume about 462 MT in FY26, demand expected at 620-630 MT by FY2029-30, per-capita use about 324 kg against a world average of about 460 kg (UltraTech AR). That is 6-7% a year. Shree's 69.3 MTPA at 80% utilisation is about 55 MT, 46% more than FY26 volume, before any new plant.

**Value chain and pricing power.** Limestone → clinker → cement → dealer → builder. Producers hold pricing power above about 80% utilisation; dealers and builders below it.

**Concentration.** Customers: none (19,362 dealers). Suppliers: imported fuel.

**For India: national build-out or one order book?** The build-out: housing first, infrastructure second. No single government customer.

**Looking back from twenty years out, a lasting business or a moment?** Lasting. India will use far more cement, and Shree will make a large share of it at a low cost. Whether that earns more than its cost of capital has not been shown in the last five years.

## Step 6 — the long horizon

**Real shift or cycle?** Both. The shift: Indian companies and banks borrow more each year, and more of it needs a rating (bank credit growth 14.5% in 2025, AR). The cycle: bond issuance swings with interest rates (flat in 2025, −18% in H1 2026, Deck).

**Closest historical parallel.** S&P and Moody's in the US over 40 years: rating agencies in a growing debt market became some of the best businesses in finance, and the scare in 2008 did not break them.

**Addressable market, growth and ceiling.** India's mutual fund assets rose from ₹55 lakh crore to about ₹83 lakh crore in two years (Call); India's corporate bond market is still small against bank lending. In research, the market is global banks' spending on outside analysis, which CRISIL does not size. Ceiling: in ratings, the speed of India's debt growth; in research, the banks' budgets.

**Value chain and pricing power.** In ratings, the issuer pays but the investor chooses, so pricing power comes from investors preferring CRISIL. In research, the big global banks hold the pricing power.

**Concentration.** Customers: 11,400+ clients in 40 countries (Call); the largest single group is S&P at 13.0%. Suppliers: people. 5,081 permanent staff (AR).

**For India: national build-out or one order book?** The build-out, for ratings. The research half depends on global banks, not India.

**Looking back from twenty years out, a lasting business or a moment?** The ratings business is lasting. The research business is a good business of this decade whose next decade is less clear.

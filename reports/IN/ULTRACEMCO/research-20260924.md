## Step 6 — the long horizon

**Real shift or cycle?** Both. The cycle: capacity outruns demand every five to six years (FY17-FY20, FY23-FY25), and the AR's own numbers point to another wave by FY28. The shift: consolidation into two national producers.

**Closest historical parallel.** The US cement industry after 2000: a handful of owners, utilisation-driven margins, returns that rarely beat the cost of capital. India has the growth the US lacked.

**Addressable market, growth and ceiling.** Industry capacity 718 MTPA at end-FY26; demand grew 6.4% in FY26; FY27 estimates 6-7.5%; demand expected to cross 620-630 MTPA by FY2029-30; per-capita use about 324 kg against about 460 kg worldwide (all AR). A 31.5% share of 630 MT is about 198 MT, which is today's installed capacity. Growth beyond that needs share gains, which is what the 240 MTPA plan is for.

**Value chain and pricing power.** Limestone → clinker → cement → dealer → builder. Producers hold pricing power above about 80% utilisation. Fuel suppliers set their own prices; railways are a regulated monopoly.

**Concentration.** Customers: none. Suppliers: imported fuel.

**For India: national build-out or one order book?** The build-out: housing first, then infrastructure. No single government customer.

**Looking back from twenty years out, a lasting business or a moment?** Lasting. India will pour more cement in the next twenty years than in the last fifty, and UltraTech will pour the largest share. Whether that share earns more than its cost of capital has not been shown in ten years.

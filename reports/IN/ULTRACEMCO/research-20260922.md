## Step 2 — the business

**One sentence.** UltraTech mines limestone, burns it into clinker, grinds that into cement, and sells 151 million tonnes a year through dealers and in bulk across India, earning the gap between the cement price and the cost of fuel, power, freight and limestone.

**Revenue split and how it has moved.** The company reports one segment. What the AR discloses: grey cement India 145.52 MMT (+13%), grey cement overseas 6.06 MMT (+10%), white cement 3.17 MMT; 465 RMC plants in 167 cities ("India's #1 RMC player"); 5,568 UltraTech Building Solutions outlets; a wall-putty acquisition (BWWPL, 6 lakh tonnes at Rajsamand); and a wires and cables plant launching in Q3 FY27. Revenue by product is not disclosed. Revenue per tonne of total cement sales (154.25 MMT) is about ₹5,738, computed, and includes RMC and other lines, so it is an upper bound on cement realisation.

**Wider or narrower than five years ago, and heading which way.** Wider. Capacity share rose from roughly a third of the industry to 27% of 700 MTPA on its own, and the top two together (UltraTech plus Adani, 306 MTPA) now hold about 44% of installed capacity (AR figures; the five-year-ago comparison is approximate). UltraTech absorbed 26.3 MTPA of acquisitions in FY25-26 and is adding 40 MTPA more. The moat is scale, and scale is growing.

**Will this moat still be here in ten years, and what would destroy it?** The scale will be there. What would destroy its value is not a competitor copying it but a competitor matching it: Adani at 155 MTPA by 2028 (reported target, secondary source) with a conglomerate's cost of capital would leave two national players of comparable reach, and duopolies in commodities compete on price when utilisation is 70-77%. The moat protects UltraTech's volume; it does not protect the industry's price.

**A historical parallel.** UltraTech itself, FY2017 to FY2020. Capacity ran ahead of demand, ROE fell from 11.7% to 8.0%, net profit fell from ₹2,714 cr to ₹2,400 cr, and the share price went from ₹3,985 (31 Mar 2017) to ₹3,998 (29 Mar 2019) to ₹3,245 (31 Mar 2020), all at year-end PEs of 40-49x (NSE closes; Scr profits). Three years of nothing for the holder, at a premium multiple, in the largest cement company in India. The re-rating that followed came from margins (OPM 18% to 26% in FY19-FY21), not from capacity. Globally, LafargeHolcim after its 2015 merger is the same story at larger scale: the biggest producer, returns near the cost of capital, a decade of flat shares.

**The short case, as its best holder would write it.** "You are paying 38 times earnings for a business that earned 11.5% on equity over ten years, and 7.6% pre-tax on the ₹46,670 cr it added to capital employed since FY22. Its largest competitor is a conglomerate that has gone from 68 to 109 MTPA in four years and says 155 by 2028. Both of them are pouring ₹50,000 cr into a market running at 70-77% of capacity. The ₹240 dividend was paid by a company whose gross debt doubled in FY25. Management has just put ₹1,800 cr into wires and cables. And by the perpetual-growth arithmetic you yourself use, a business earning its cost of capital deserves 8 to 10 times earnings, not 38."

**My own biases on this name.** The screen produced UltraTech as the only clean pass of eleven, which is survivorship of a rules screen, not evidence of value. "Largest in India" is a story that reads well and I have leaned on it in the moat section. I am anchored on the year-end PE median of 40x as "normal", when the same table shows 16x in March 2020. And this is an A-grade name: the quantity of material makes it feel more certain than an 11% ROE business is.

**India-specific checks.** Related-party transactions: the AR states there were no material transactions under section 188 and all were at arm's length with audit committee approval; dealings with Grasim (parent, 57% owner) and group companies were not itemised in this pass. Auditor: KKC & Associates LLP completes its second term at the 26th AGM and Deloitte Haskins & Sells LLP was approved as statutory auditor (AR); this is rotation, and no qualification or resignation appears. Promoter pledge: 0.0%. Regulated tariffs: none directly, but railway freight and state mining levies are set by government. Single government customer: no. Land and approvals: the 40 MTPA expansion needs mining and environmental clearances; contingent liabilities assumed from India Cements (₹556.09 cr) and Kesoram (₹266.14 cr) per the AR. The 2016 competition-law cartelisation penalty on the cement industry remains a known contingent item; its current status was not verified in this pass.

**Where am I most likely to be wrong, and why would a smart person be short?** I am most likely wrong about the multiple: I have treated 38x as the market's considered view of a consolidator, when the model says a 12% ROIC business deserves a fifth of that. A smart person is short because the growth needed to justify the price (30% a year for ten years at the model exit multiple, Step 7) has never been achieved by this company or this industry.

## Step 6 — the long horizon

**Real shift or cycle?** Both. The cycle is the usual one: capacity runs ahead of demand every five or six years, margins compress, then recover (FY17-FY20, then FY23-FY25). The shift is consolidation: the top two now hold about 44% of installed capacity, and the AR's own industry framing is that "consolidations and ongoing capacity additions" are the competitive fact. Consolidation should raise pricing discipline over time. So far it has raised capex.

**Closest historical parallel.** The US cement industry after 2000: consolidation into a handful of global owners, then two decades of utilisation-driven margins and returns that rarely beat the cost of capital, with the best returns going to whoever had the lowest logistics cost to the fastest-growing region. India has the growth the US lacked, which is the case for optimism.

**Addressable market, growth curve, ceiling.** Industry volume about 462 MMT in FY26 (+6-7%), demand expected to cross 620-630 MTPA by FY2029-30, per-capita consumption about 324 kg against a global average of about 460 kg, installed capacity about 700 MTPA (all AR). That is roughly 6-7% volume growth a year for the decade. A 31.5% share of a 630 MMT market is about 198 MMT, which is UltraTech's installed capacity in FY26. Growth beyond that needs share gain, which needs the 240 MTPA.

**Value chain and pricing power.** Limestone (owned leases) → clinker → cement → dealer → builder. Pricing power sits with nobody for long: producers when utilisation is above 80%, dealers and buyers below it. Suppliers of fuel are dollar price-setters; railways are a regulated monopoly; UltraTech has moved to 35.8% green power to own more of its energy.

**Concentration.** Customers: none; over 5,500 own outlets and a national dealer network (count not disclosed). Suppliers: imported pet coke and coal are the concentration.

**For India: national build-out or one order book?** The build-out. Housing is the largest demand segment (AR), infrastructure second; there is no single government customer. Government capex sets the pace of the infrastructure half.

**Looking back from twenty years out, is this a lasting business or a moment?** Lasting. India will pour more cement in the next twenty years than in the last fifty, and UltraTech will pour the largest share of it. Whether the owner of that share earns more than the cost of capital on it is the open question, and it has not been answered in the last ten years.

### Ten-year view (`terminal_value.py`)

**Audit before publication** (`terminal_value.py audit --currency INR --r 0.12 --roic 0.12 --g 0.03,0.04,0.05 --rf 0.0705 --beta 1.0`): passed all three constraints. C1 currency: r 12% inside the INR band 11-14%, g ceiling 5%, rf 7.05%. C2 denominator: r − g = 9.0 / 8.0 / 7.0 points for g = 3 / 4 / 5%, all above the 5-point floor; exit PE 8.3x in each case and insensitive to g because ROIC equals r. C3 discrete risks: Adani price war → scenario; India Cements and Kesoram integration shortfall → scenario; limestone lease or mining tax change → probability; wires and cables diversification → scenario; **MD transition Jan 2027 → unmodelled** (the tool requires this to be stated in the limits). Beta 1.0. The same audit passed at r = 11% (exit PE 9.4-9.7x) and r = 14% (6.5-6.8x). A run at r = 10% was rejected by the tool as outside the INR band, correctly.

**Steady-state ROIC assumption:** 12%, between the AR's FY26 ROCE of 11.8% and Screener's 13%, and above stockanalysis's 9.6% ROIC. Using 15% (the best UltraTech reached in FY22 on SA's series was 13.9%) is shown as the upside case.

|---|---|---|---|
| 12% | 3-5% | 12% | 8.3x |
| 15% | 4% | 12% | 9.2x |
| 20% | 4% | 12% | 10.0x |

| 15% | 4% | 11% | 10.5x |
| 12% | 5% | 14% | 6.5x |
| 15% | 5% | 14% | 7.4x |

| Profit growth to FY36 | FY36 profit | Exit 8.3x (ROIC 12%) | Exit 9.2x (ROIC 15%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5% a year | ₹13,337 cr | −9.6% | −8.6% |
| 10% a year | ₹21,238 cr | −5.3% | −4.3% |
| 15% a year | ₹33,125 cr | −1.0% | 0.0% |

r used: 12%, paired with a g ceiling of 5%. At r = 11% the exit PE rises to 9.5x and the 15%-growth IRR becomes about +0.5%; at r = 14% it falls to 6.5x and every path is more negative. **The ranking and the sign hold at both ends of the band.** r − g width: 7-9 points at 12%, 6-8 at 11%, 9-11 at 14%. Discrete risks left unmodelled: the MD transition. Discrete risks given scenarios rather than a discount-rate loading: the Adani price war and the acquisition integration, both of which are the bear and base paths above.

**If r is wrong by 2 points, does the conclusion flip?** No. At 11% and at 14% the ten-year IRR is negative or zero on every growth path up to 15%. The conclusion is about the business earning its cost of capital, not about the discount rate.

**If the market shut for five years, would this price be acceptable?** On the business, yes: net debt/EBITDA 0.94x, FCF positive ten years running, a 31% share of a growing market. On the price, only if profit compounds above 15% and the market reopens at today's multiple. That is a bet on the multiple.

> **This report does not say buy or sell.** It gives the range, the thesis lines, and what would break them. The call is Shivay's.

### Thesis lines — what must stay true

1. **EBITDA per tonne stays above ₹1,100** through the FY27-FY29 capacity additions (FY26: ₹1,141; FY25: ₹979). Quarterly proxy: consolidated OPM at or above 18% (Q1 FY27: 20%).
2. **India Cements reaches a double-digit operating margin by FY28 and holds it** (FY25 −8%, FY26 9%, Screener). If the acquired plants cannot earn the group margin, the acquisition model is broken.
3. **Grey cement volume grows at least 8% a year** (FY26 +13%), which means UltraTech keeps gaining share while the industry grows 6-7%.
4. **Net debt to EBITDA stays below 1.5x** while the ₹16,000 cr expansion is spent (FY26 0.94x; FY25 1.33x).

### Red lines — events that reopen the case

- Promoter pledge above zero.
- A second consecutive year of ROCE below 10% (AR basis; FY26 11.8%).
- Two consecutive quarters of consolidated OPM below 15%, which is EBITDA/t near ₹900.
- Wires and cables spend rising above the approved ₹1,800 cr, or a second diversification outside building materials.
- A material related-party transaction with a group company, an auditor qualification, or an unexplained auditor exit.
- A capacity target raised again before utilisation is back above 80%.

### How confident the analysis is, and how certain the business is

These are different things. **Analysis confidence: high.** Every figure has two sources or a primary document; the tools passed; the cross-validation flags were traced to definitional differences. **Business certainty: moderate at best.** This is a cyclical, capital-heavy commodity producer whose ten-year return on equity sits at its cost of capital. An A-grade file on a B-grade business.

### Bias check, re-read at the end

- **Is my certainty coming from the business or from the material?** From the material. The 327-page report and twelve years of clean data made the numbers feel solid; the numbers themselves say 11% ROE.
- **If half the material vanished, would the conclusion change?** No. The conclusion rests on five numbers: ROE 11.5%, incremental return 7.6%, PE 38x, model exit PE 8-10x, growth needed 30%. All are in Screener and the AR.
- **Does this read like the consensus? Where is the edge?** It does not. The consensus (SA forward PE 34x, analyst targets) treats 35-40x as normal for the leader. The edge, if it is one, is the perpetual-growth arithmetic that the fork's rules force and most coverage skips.
- **Could a business this good be this quiet?** It is not quiet. It is the most covered cement company in India, which is exactly the A-grade trap.

# Part 1 — the gates

## Gate 1 — can I understand this business? ★★★★ (4)

- **One sentence:** CAMS does the back office for mutual funds — accepting investments, redemptions and SIP instalments, keeping every investor's account, paying distributors — and the fund houses pay it mostly a fee on the assets it services, plus fees per transaction.
- **In ten years:** the same record-keeping for mutual funds, plus the same work for alternative funds, insurance policies (the insurance repository), pensions (NPS record-keeping), KYC records (the KRA) and payments (CAMSPay).
- **The variables that decide it:** (1) how fast mutual fund assets grow, which is markets plus new money; (2) how much the fee per rupee falls each year, through telescopic pricing, renewals and SEBI's limits on what funds may charge; (3) whether it keeps its large clients against KFintech.
- **Source:** the AR, the prospectus and three earnings calls, not a tip.

**Why profit was flat in FY2026.** Owners' profit rose 1.2% (₹470.19 cr → ₹476.01 cr) on revenue up 6.6% (AR). Four reasons, from the AR and the calls:
1. **A price cut.** A "price reset" agreed with clients in Jan 2025 came into the fees in Q4 FY25 and Q1 FY26 (Q3 FY26 call). The company did not name the client. So revenue grew much less than assets serviced.
2. **Weak markets for part of the year.** Assets grew only "a little more than 1%" in Q4 FY26 (Q4 FY26 call).
3. **Depreciation up 27.1%**, from ₹77.72 cr to ₹98.77 cr, from new equipment and the platform rebuild (AR).
4. **A one-time labour-code charge** of about ₹2.5-3 cr, mostly gratuity, when the new labour codes took effect on 21 Nov 2025 (Q3 FY26 call; AR note on employee costs).

The price reset is now "in the base" (Q1 FY27 call), and Q1 FY27 profit rose 17.3%.

**One-time or repeat?** Repeat. The fee on assets is charged every day on the money in the funds. There are no one-time sales in the base: "you will not find a single instance" (MD, Q1 FY27 call).

**Customer lock-in.** High but not absolute. Contracts run until one side ends them on 3-6 months' notice (prospectus). The fund houses renegotiate price periodically: "a few midsized AMCs up for renewal" in FY27, "impact ... very muted" (Q1 FY27 call).

**Customer concentration.** The top five clients gave 66.9%, 67.1%, 67.4% and 70.9% of revenue in FY18, FY19, FY20 and Q1 FY21 (prospectus). No later figure is published; the AR says only that "within MF the revenue is concentrated within a group of top clients" and lists concentration as a key risk. At the listing, CAMS served four of the five largest fund houses — SBI, HDFC, ICICI Prudential and Aditya Birla Sun Life (prospectus). Today it serves 9 of the 15 largest (Deck).

KFintech also has share-registry and overseas businesses, so the margins are not strictly like for like. The reason for CAMS's lead is scale: the larger funds on one platform.

**What is good about this business, in one sentence:** it earns a small, recurring cut of a pool of savings that has grown about 20% a year for a decade, and almost all of the profit comes back as cash.

**A historical parallel.** DST Systems in the US was for decades the largest record-keeper for American mutual funds, with a dominant share and sticky clients. Its growth slowed as fund houses consolidated, moved to cheaper passive products and pressed fees down, and it was bought by SS&C in 2018. Dominance in fund record-keeping did not protect the fee per account.

**The short case, as its best holder would write it.** "You are paying 36 times earnings for a company whose price goes down by contract every year. Its customers are a few very large fund houses, and every one of them can leave on three to six months' notice. Profit did not grow in FY2026 because one renewal cut its fees. SEBI has just cut what funds may charge again, and the fund houses will pass that on. Passive funds, which pay almost nothing, are growing. Foreign funds have cut their holding from 57.6% to 44.9% in eighteen months. And the growth businesses it talks about earn a 13% margin."

**My own biases on this name.** The screen called it the "moderate-certainty grower", and I start leaning toward agreeing. The 67% share and the 45% margin are round, reassuring numbers, and I am in danger of treating a duopoly as a monopoly: the price cuts show the clients hold the pricing power. And the base case beats the index fund by only 1.4 points, so a small change in my growth guess changes the answer.

**India-specific checks.** Related parties: MF Central Technologies is a 50-50 joint venture with KFintech (share of loss ₹0.76 cr in FY26, AR). Auditor: S.R. Batliboi, unmodified opinion. Pledge: not applicable, no promoter. Regulated prices: not directly, but SEBI sets what funds may charge, and an industry-wide KRA rate cut of about 29% took effect on 1 Apr 2026 (Deck; Q1 FY27 call). Single government customer: no. Land and approvals: not material.

**Where am I most likely to be wrong, and why would a smart person be short?** Wrong if the fee per rupee falls much faster than 3% a year once clients see what SEBI's cut does to their own margins. A smart person is short because FY2026 already showed what one renewal does, and the price still assumes the market pays 36x in ten years.

## Step 6 — the long horizon

**Real shift or cycle?** A shift, with cycles on top. Indians are moving savings from bank deposits and gold into mutual funds; assets grew about 20% a year over the last ten years, 12-13 points from market gains and 7-8 from new money (MD, Q3 FY26 call). Markets add cycles: FY23 and parts of FY26 were flat.

**Closest historical parallel.** US fund record-keeping (DST, above): a dominant record-keeper in a fast-growing fund market kept its share for decades, but the fee per account shrank.

**Addressable market.** Industry average assets ₹83.1 lakh crore in Q1 FY27 (Deck). CAMS serves 4.85 crore unique investors and 11.62 crore folios (Deck). New pools: specialised investment funds (11 launched, over ₹10,000 cr), GIFT City retail funds (₹750 cr), alternative funds (₹3.2 lakh crore serviced) (Deck).

**Value chain and pricing power.** SEBI → fund houses → RTA. SEBI sets what funds may charge investors; the fund houses, which are few and large, set what they pay CAMS. CAMS holds the least pricing power of the three.

**Concentration.** Customers: high, a few large fund houses. Suppliers: cloud and data-centre providers; the MD warned costs there "are becoming expensive faster than we thought" (Q1 FY27 call). Cloud commitments for the rebuild: ₹90.48 cr over four years (AR note 36).

**For India: build-out or one order book?** The build-out: household savings moving into markets. No government customer.

**Looking back from twenty years out, a lasting business or a moment?** Lasting, as the plumbing of Indian mutual funds. Whether shareholders do well depends on the fee per rupee falling slower than assets grow.

### Ten-year view (`terminal_value.py`)

The same audit passed at r = 11% (exit PE 11.4-14.3x) and r = 14% (8.3-9.5x).

Tool note: the risk labels were passed in English and accepted. The tool source lists `scenario`, `tail`, `probability` and `unmodelled` as valid; the Chinese-only limit found on 23 Sep no longer applies.

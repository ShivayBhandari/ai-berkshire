## Short analysis — IRCTC

**The business in one line.** IRCTC is the Indian Railways' sole online ticket seller, and also runs train and station catering, Rail Neer bottled water and rail tourism. It earns a convenience fee per ticket and a margin on catering and tourism.

**Financial quality.** Standalone sales went from ₹3,541 cr (FY2023) to ₹5,215 cr (FY2026); net profit from ₹1,006 cr to ₹1,393 cr, 11.5% a year over three years. The five-year figures start from the FY2021 lockdown and overstate growth. Operating margin has come down from 36% (FY2023) to 32% (FY2026) as lower-margin catering grew. ROE is 35.0%, and there is almost no debt. Cash from operations was 0.79 times profit over five years. The most important change in the last two years is slower growth: profit rose 5.9% in FY2026.

**Moat.** Licence. IRCTC is the Railways' only authorised online ticketing channel. That is a strong moat, but the Railways own it and can change the terms. Stable. 4 stars.

**Top 3 risks.**
1. Policy. The Railways decide the convenience fee and IRCTC's share of it.
2. Mix. Catering and tourism grow faster and earn less, so the margin keeps slipping.
3. Growth. Ticketing volume grows with rail travel, which is slow; three-year profit growth is 11.5%.

**Valuation now.** PE 27.2x (stockanalysis 26.96x), PB 8.7x (₹470 / ₹53.8 book), dividend yield 1.92%. The PE sits at the bottom of its own range (26.8-250.9x, median 56.5x) and at the peer median of 27.2x. **Fair.**

**Into the final three?** Yes. Highest ROE in the sector, no debt, and a licence nobody else holds.

## Short analysis — RailTel

**The business in one line.** RailTel lays and leases optical fibre along railway tracks, sells bandwidth and data-centre services, and does IT and telecom project work for government bodies, paid per contract.

**Financial quality.** Standalone sales grew from ₹1,337 cr (FY2021) to ₹4,277 cr (FY2026), 26.2% a year; net profit from ₹140 cr to ₹346 cr, 19.8% a year. ROE has risen three years running to 16.2%. There is almost no debt. The most important change is the mix: project work has grown fastest, and operating margin fell from 24% (FY2022) to 15% (FY2026). Free cash flow was −₹70 cr in FY2025 and ₹44 cr in FY2026, much weaker than earlier years.

**Moat.** Right of way. RailTel has exclusive access to lay fibre along the railway network, which a telecom company cannot easily copy. The project business has no moat: it is won on tender. 3 stars, narrowing as project work takes a bigger share.

**Top 3 risks.**
1. Margin. More low-margin project work pulls the operating margin down.
2. Cash. Project work ties up working capital; free cash flow is already thin.
3. Customer. Most revenue comes from the Railways and other government bodies.

**Valuation now.** PE 24.5x (stockanalysis 24.46x), PB 3.7x (₹264 / ₹70.5 book), dividend yield 1.23%. Below its own median of 28.8x and below the peer median. **Fair.**

**Into the final three?** Yes, as the grower.

## Short analysis — RITES

**The business in one line.** RITES sells engineering consultancy, quality inspection, turnkey projects, rolling-stock exports and locomotive leasing, mostly to the Railways and other government bodies.

**Financial quality.** Consolidated sales peaked at ₹2,662 cr (FY2022) and were ₹2,415 cr in FY2026. Net profit fell from ₹571 cr (FY2023) to ₹454 cr (FY2026). Operating margin slipped from 29% (FY2023) to 24% (FY2026). ROE is still 17.1% and there is no debt. The most important change in the last two years is shrinking profit, as exports and turnkey work fell.

**Moat.** A relationship and certification edge in railway inspection and consultancy. The rest is tendered work. 3 stars, not widening.

**Top 3 risks.**
1. Profit has fallen for three years, and the order book was not checked in this screen.
2. Exports are lumpy; one large order moves a year's profit.
3. Customer concentration in government bodies.

**Valuation now.** PE 23.4x (stockanalysis 23.46x), PB 3.7x (₹204 / ₹55.8 book), dividend yield 3.90%. 50% above its own median of 15.6x, below the peer median. **Expensive** against its own history.

**Into the final three?** Yes, but as the one to watch, not a pick. It survives on quality and balance sheet; the numbers are going the wrong way.

### How each check was computed

Same method as the 22 Sep screens.

| # | Check | How |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10-year average ROE | net profit / average of opening and closing (equity capital + reserves), each year FY2017-FY2026, then averaged |
| 2 | 5-year cumulative FCF | Screener's Free Cash Flow row summed FY2022-FY2026 |
| 3 | Interest cover | (profit before tax + interest) / interest, FY2026 |
| 4 | Operating margin | Screener OPM %, ten-year average |
| 5 | OCF / net profit | five-year cash from operations / five-year net profit, FY2022-FY2026 |
| 6 | Net margin | net profit / sales each year, ten-year average |
| 7 | Dilution | equity capital, FY2026 against FY2021. Where a bonus issue moved the capital, the share count from net profit / EPS is used instead, and the row says so |
| 8 | Pledge / promoter | pledge from Trendlyne's shareholding page for the names that passed checks 1-7; promoter % Sep 2023 → Jun 2026 from Screener. Names eliminated on checks 1-7 were not searched and stay NA |

## Short analysis — HAL

**The business in one line.** HAL designs, builds and overhauls military aircraft, helicopters and aero-engines, almost entirely for the Indian armed forces, paid by the Ministry of Defence against contract milestones, often with large advances.

**Financial quality.** Sales grew from ₹22,755 cr (FY2021) to ₹33,089 cr (FY2026), 7.8% a year. Net profit grew 23.0% a year over the same five years, to ₹9,116 cr. Operating margin averaged 24.7% over ten years. ROE averaged 24.3%, and was 24.0% in FY2026. Five-year cash from operations was 1.44 times profit, and there is no debt. The most important change of the last two years is the order book: ₹2.54 lakh crore at the end of FY2026, up from ₹1.89 lakh crore a year earlier, after ₹97,028 cr of new orders including 97 Tejas Mk1A (Whalesbook, one source — not checked against HAL's own filing). That is about 7.7 years of FY2026 sales.

One number to hold against the profit growth: other income was ₹3,743 cr in FY2026 (sum of the four quarters), about 30% of profit before tax. Much of it is interest on cash that customer advances fund; the split was not checked in the annual report.

**Moat.** Licence and regulatory barrier, plus scale. HAL is the only Indian maker of combat aircraft and military helicopters, and the design and certification lead takes decades. It is narrowing at the edges: private firms (Tata, L&T) are entering transport aircraft and parts, and the government has opened some programmes to private bidders. 4 stars.

**Top 3 risks.**
1. One customer sets the price. Contracts are largely on nomination and cost-plus, so a policy change moves the margin.
2. Engine supply. GE F404 engines for Tejas Mk1A arrived late; seven received, ten more expected before November 2026, and first-squadron delivery is now targeted by March 2027.
3. Profit quality. 30% of pre-tax profit is other income, which falls if the cash pile shrinks.

**Valuation now.** PE 34.4x (stockanalysis 34.44x), PB 7.8x (₹4,800 / ₹614 book), dividend yield 0.94%. The PE is 2.4 times its own ten-year median of 14.4x, and near the top of its 6.0-48.7x range. It is the cheapest name in the group against a peer median of 82.7x. **Expensive** against its own history, **fair** against peers.

**Into the final three?** Yes. It is the only name that passed both steps.
